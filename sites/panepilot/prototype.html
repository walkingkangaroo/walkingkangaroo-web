      The whole page, in order

      One page and a 404. Privacy and terms stay in the app, where Google's consent screen and the subscription law already point.

      1. Header. Lockup, How it works, Pricing, Questions, Sign in, and "Start your free trial" as the one filled button.
      2. Hero. The headline, one line, the trial button with its terms, and the real app in a wide browser window: the week calendar, a quote and a paid invoice.
      3. Try each step of the round. Five tabs: Quoting, Scheduling, Visits, Invoicing and Workers' pay, each with a small working copy of that part of the app.
      4. Made for window cleaning. What the general tools don't do: your own surcharges that stack on any line; runs of shops in your order, some every 2nd or 3rd run; every n weeks or months; reminders for customers who like to be asked first; access and hazard notes; a glass-scratch acknowledgement signed by typed name; "Invoice" with no GST line for a business that isn't registered.
      5. Your workers. The time card. They sign in with their own Google or Microsoft account or an emailed code, see only their own visits, time and estimated pay. No cost per worker.
      6. Built by a window cleaner. The story, drafted by Claude and rewritten by Grant, naming Clare Valley Window Cleaning. No photo.
      7. Pricing, on a Deep Water band: one plan, A$49 a month, no GST, unlimited workers, every feature, 30 days free with no card, cancel online. A free setup call, and a spreadsheet import for your customers.
      8. What it doesn't do yet (kept): card payments, Xero or MYOB, route planning, an App Store app.
      9. Questions. Where my data is kept, bringing customers from Fergus or a spreadsheet, whether workers pay, phones, GST, cancelling, and what happens to my data if I stop.
      10. Footer. Privacy, terms, a contact address (to come), and the Walking Kangaroo signature.

      What we take from the three reference sites

      fergus.com

      Take: the plain tradie voice, and an FAQ that answers the money questions (trial, no setup fee, no lock-in).

      Leave: review badges, "25,000+ businesses", a promo banner, the sign-up form four times over.

      Jobber, window cleaning

      Take: organising by the job (quote, schedule, do the work, get paid) and the window-specific details like window counts.

      Leave: the same feature blocks twice, a pressure-washing video, generic home-service copy.

      Housecall Pro, window cleaning

      Take: quotes "built around panes", storefront rounds, an FAQ written for window cleaners.

      Leave: the growth-stats row, testimonials from carpet cleaners, a gift-card pop-up.

      All three say "window cleaning" on a home-services page. PanePilot can show it: the pane count, the run of shops, the ladder on the top windows, an invoice with no GST line.
